== Building a Centre of Excellence

image::AI-Images/Centre%20of%20Excellence%2001.png[float=center,align=center]

Here's the thing: most organisations don't have a technology problem. They have a "nobody's in charge of how we use it" problem. One department is running its own cloud subscriptions, another is building low-code apps nobody has heard of, a third is quietly feeding customer data into an AI tool, and the data team is trying to make sense of it all.

A Centre of Excellence (CoE) fixes that. It's a central hub for technology expertise, processes and knowledge across your organisation – covering cloud, low-code, data, AI and agents, and the T-Minus-15 way of working that ties them together. Think of it as mission control: it doesn't fly every rocket, but it makes sure every launch is safe, coordinated and pointed in the right direction. This chapter explores why a CoE matters and how to set one up.

You need a central unit to steer adoption and make full use of what you're paying for. A good CoE helps you get the most from your technology investments, cut costs and stay ahead of the competition. It makes sure the work is done consistently and securely across the organisation, reducing the risk of incidents – whether that's a misconfigured cloud resource or an agent with far too much access.

Building a CoE takes a dedicated team of experts who understand the organisation's technology strategy and the tools, processes and best practices needed to support it. Structure your CoE to deliver:

* *Governance* – Policies and procedures that keep the use of cloud, low-code, data and AI consistent, secure and compliant with industry regulations. That includes guardrails for AI agents: what they can touch, and who reviews their work.
* *Architecture* – Making sure solutions, from cloud infrastructure to low-code apps and data platforms, are designed and deployed to meet the organisation's needs, and are scalable, secure and cost-effective.
* *Operations* – Ongoing support and maintenance for your platforms, keeping the organisation up to date with the latest cloud services, low-code releases and AI models.
* *Education and training* – Ongoing training so staff have the skills and knowledge to use these tools well, from provisioning cloud resources to working safely alongside AI agents.
* *Innovation* – Encouraging and promoting innovation across the organisation, using cloud, low-code, data and AI to drive business growth and success.

=== Strategic Vision

Creating a Centre of Excellence is a significant undertaking, so make sure its delivery is aligned with the strategic themes and vision of the business. The CoE shouldn't be just another IT initiative. It should be tied to the overall business goals and priorities, and that means representation from across the business.

How do you keep it aligned? Regularly review the strategic themes of the business and assess the CoE's progress towards them. That gives the CoE the chance to adjust course so it keeps delivering value in line with the business's priorities. And always ask about the "bang for buck" when choosing what to take on: focus effort on delivering the most value in the shortest amount of time.

=== Idea Portal

Your best ideas are probably already in someone's head – a customer, a team member, someone in finance who's sick of re-keying the same spreadsheet. Collecting them is crucial for innovation and continuous improvement. An Ideas Portal gives everyone one place to submit suggestions and proposals, creating a collaborative environment that encourages creativity and participation. It's a direct line between the business and its stakeholders, so teams can understand what customers actually need and act on it in good time. Review and prioritise the ideas regularly, and you'll spot new trends, areas for improvement and opportunities for growth early. Encourage people to share, and you build a culture of innovation that leads to better outcomes and happier customers.

=== Portfolio Management

Portfolio management keeps the CoE honest. It makes sure the work being done lines up with the strategic themes of the business. Assess the impact versus cost of each initiative in the portfolio to work out which will deliver the most value, then organise the work into a portfolio roadmap that sets out the priorities and keeps everyone focused on the bigger picture. In T-Minus-15 terms, this is where Plan earns its keep. Review the portfolio regularly and adjust as the business environment and market conditions change. That way, the CoE keeps delivering maximum value to the business.

=== Architecture & Standards

The Architecture & Standards group exists to help the agile delivery squads succeed. By creating frameworks, blueprints, reusable templates and standards – for cloud landing zones, low-code environments, data models and agent guardrails alike – it helps squads deliver high-value, high-quality solutions at a high cadence. Make no mistake: this group can be the difference between teams getting solutions to production quickly and teams bogged down by a wall of rigid standards. Take a facilitative approach, not a gatekeeping one. Enable teams to deliver value fast while still making sure solutions are high quality and meet every necessary requirement, and the Architecture & Standards group helps the company achieve its strategic vision.

=== Communities of Practice

The CoE should be responsible for creating Communities of Practice (CoP) around topics such as AI, low-code, security, automation, testing, DevOps, and agile. These CoPs should bring together experts from across the squads and organisation to share knowledge and best practices.

=== Innovation Squad

The innovation squad is the CoE's test pilot. Its job is to develop new and innovative solutions. Using the principles of lean startup, the squad tests and validates an epic hypothesis before a large investment of time and resources is made. That lets the team fail fast and avoid costly mistakes, and gives the business the chance to pivot and refine its strategy quickly. AI agents help here: a prototype to test a hypothesis can be in front of users in hours rather than weeks. By proving or disproving the hypothesis in a controlled and measured way, the innovation squad keeps the CoE aligned with the strategic themes of the business and delivering value efficiently.

=== Agile Delivery Squads

The agile delivery squads are where the rubber meets the launchpad. They're responsible for delivering high-value, high-quality solutions at a high cadence, from an idea all the way through to production. They do it by following the T-Minus-15 lifecycle: Assemble the crew, then work iteratively through Plan → Prep → Design → Engineer → Test → Operate. Each squad is made up of people with specialised roles for each of these stages – increasingly working alongside AI agents – so they can deliver features every sprint. This autonomous delivery model keeps squads efficient and effective, delivering solutions that line up with the strategic themes of the business. With clear roles and stages, they can focus on delivering value to the customer.

=== Iterative Improvement

Continuous, iterative improvement is critical to any Centre of Excellence. The CoE should keep making small, iterative changes to people, process and technology to drive positive change. This approach, known as Kaizen, is a key part of effective organisational change. But you also need a clear picture of what "good" looks like. Define success, and the CoE can measure progress, spot areas for improvement and adjust its approach as needed. Combine a focus on continuous improvement with a clear definition of success, and the CoE's efforts stay aligned with the strategic vision of the organisation and deliver value in line with the company's goals and priorities.

=== Performance of the CoE

The CoE should also use metrics to measure its performance, including DORA (State of DevOps) metrics to track the speed and efficiency of the delivery process. But don't stop at the numbers. Team happiness matters just as much when making decisions: a happy team is more likely to deliver value to customers, leading to a higher customer Net Promoter Score (NPS).
